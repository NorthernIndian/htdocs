Race

  • Start Time: 08:00 AM
  • Shoes: Brooks Catamount.
  • Watch: Garmin Forerunner 255.
  • Apparel: Nike Flex 7" shorts, old race shirt, Ceile hat, and Solomon Active Skin 8
  • Supplements: (9) Huma and Stinger Gels with Skratch.
  • Hydration: (5) 500ml flasks all filled with Skratch.
  • Aid Stations: Only used the aid stations for water.
  • Potty Breaks: The 23k aid station did not have a porta potty, so I had to squat over a log in the forest.

Ever since registering months earlier, this race had been percolating in the back of my mind. I thought that there was a chance to make the podium if things went well. I was also excited to compete with my friend Dave Snively.

The race started with some light rain which was very nice as I like it cool. Dave took off like a rocket and so did a few other guys. Adrenaline got the better of me and I too pushed a bit. I knew this wasn't sustainable because my heart rate was way higher than I wanted it to be.

I ended up running with another guy up to the Powderface ridge which wasn't smart because he was pushing the pace and I was going a bit faster than I wanted too. I ended up slowing down and letting him go, but later I passed him on the downhill section.

When heading down Powderface, the jarring motion upset my stomach and I knew that I would have to shit sooner or later. I decided that it would be at the 23k aid station. Unfortunately, there wasn't a porta potty at the aid station, so I ran 200m past the aid station and pooped in the bush. Thank God I brought toilet paper - one of the better decisions I made all day!

The section between the 23k and 32k aid station was quiet and daunting. I didn't see another soul. I slipped at one point and cut my finger.

I looked at my watch around 28k and realized that I had been running for 3 hours and 15 minutes - the same time it took me to run the road marathon a month earlier. At this point I knew I was in for some pain.

I started struggling with gels and hydration after 30k - I believe I was behind on hydration and it was affecting how the gels were going down.

At the 32k air station, I got some plain water and chugged it. I was dehydrated based on how thirsty I was.

The next 6k was tough. The sun came out just as I was ascending the mountain and it got muggy quick. At 38k I tried to consume another gel but it didn't sit right and 100m later I started vomitting hard. I actually felt better after everything came out.

The last few kilometers down the mountain was a slog. I tried to keep up the pace because someone was on my heels, but they passed me in the final stretch.

It was a massive relief to cross the finish line and see my family.

Post-race

  • Finish Time: 04:54:23

This was one of those races that kicked my ass. After finishing I had the shakes and my lips went blue. I found it difficult to eat anything and I felt like sleeping.

Dave crushed it and finished 2nd overall!

This was probably one of the hardest races that I've done - it hurt my soul! But I will probably be back next year...

Lessons Learned

  • Toilet paper is invaluable.
  • Stay ahead of hydration.
  • Run your own race and don't let adrenaline dictate effort.
  • Pay more attention to electrolytes in these longer races.
  • Pay more attention to race fueling - Glucose to fructose ratio might have been off.
