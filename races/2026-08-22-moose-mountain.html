Race

  • Start Time: 08:30 AM

It was a cool morning with a chance of showers—pretty much perfect racing weather for me. I was feeling really good and excited to race. I met up with Kristen at the start line and was excited for her to take on her first 50K.

I started out according to plan, but right away I noticed how much better I felt compared to previous years. I just felt stronger. The vert seemed so much more manageable and nowhere near as daunting as it had in the past.

The descent was an absolute blast. I was passing people constantly and felt completely in control. I kept telling myself that I was a dump truck and could just run straight over all the rugged terrain without worrying about it. Somehow it worked. It felt like I was floating down the mountain.

I backed off quite a bit on the Ridgeback loop because I kept reminding myself that I was supposed to be treating this more like an 80K effort. There were a lot of run-walk sections, and at one point I went roughly 15 km without seeing another runner. It was a strangely quiet part of the race.

Around this time the sun came out and it started getting warmer and more humid.

I reached the 36K aid station in great shape and still feeling really good. I tried to eat some solid food, but my mouth was too dry to get much down, so I decided to stick with the gels that had been working well all day.

The first few kilometres of the Telephone loop were incredible. I felt amazing and was running fast. Up to that point, the nutrition had been working really well and I felt completely dialed in.

Then, somewhere around 43K, that familiar nausea started creeping in. Eventually I couldn't take in any more gels, water, or sodium, and my pace began to slow. Despite that, I was still moving reasonably well and continued passing people.

At about 48K I finally vomited. Somehow, I was still able to keep pushing afterward and continued picking people off on the way to the finish.

I finished tired, but strong. Overall, I was really happy with the race. More than anything, I came away feeling like a much stronger trail runner than I had been in previous years. The climbing felt easier, the technical descending felt natural, and for the first 43K I felt fantastic.

The one obvious issue was nutrition. I had been taking in roughly 90–95 g of carbs per hour, and while it worked extremely well for most of the race, the late-race nausea and vomiting was becoming a familiar pattern. It left me wondering whether backing that down to around 75–80 g per hour might be the sweet spot for longer races.

Overall, this was a really fun race minus the vomiting. I felt stronger on the climbs and descents than I had in previous years and was still able to finish well despite the nausea late in the race. The biggest thing I need to figure out is what's happening with my stomach. The problem seems to show up later in races, particularly as the temperature rises. More troubleshooting needed.

Lessons Learned

  • A controlled effort early pays dividends later in the race. I still had plenty of legs left for the final sections.
  • Heat and humidity seem to increase my risk of late-race nausea and GI problems.
  • 90–95 g of carbs per hour worked extremely well for most of the race, but may be more than my gut can reliably tolerate over 5–6+ hours. Experiment with 75–80 g/hr.
  • Slowing down briefly after taking a gel seemed to help with digestion and absorption.
  • Carrying only the water and nutrition needed between aid stations worked well and reduced unnecessary weight.
  • My trail running has improved significantly. The climbing felt more manageable and I was much more confident attacking technical descents.
