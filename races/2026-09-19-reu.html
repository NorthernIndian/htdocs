I went into this race still recovering from a cough. I was a little hacky, but otherwise my body felt great and I was ready to go.

The first loop was about 20K and went almost perfectly. I kept the effort controlled and finished the loop feeling great. At the aid station, I dropped my vest and grabbed my preloaded waist belt for the river loop.

The river loop ended up being my favourite loop of the race. It had everything—mud, a river crossing, and some really fun running. I even got lucky and found a deer antler while crossing the river. I carried it with me and finished the loop still feeling strong.

Next up was the Homestead loop, a meandering section through the forest that started with some punchy hills. The temperature was beginning to climb, but I was still moving well and running a strong pace.

Near the end of the loop, though, that familiar nausea started creeping in. I tried spacing my gels out a little more, but I just felt so damn hot. Once we hit the north bank of the river, it felt like a sauna. For the first time all day, I seriously felt like quitting. I passed another runner who told me he was "fucking done"—and he literally quit.

I made it back to the aid station at around 48K and took a 20–30 minute break. At that point, the priority was simply to cool myself down and get some fluids and calories back in.

I eventually headed out for the second Homestead loop, but there was a lot more running and walking this time around. I felt terrible and it was still so damn hot. At the 58K aid station, I took another extended break to try to bring myself back to life. I ate some potatoes and perogies and sipped on Pepsi. Surprisingly, I left the aid station feeling quite a bit better—but that burst of energy only lasted about 4K.

By the time I reached the 68K aid station, I felt like shit again and needed another extended break.

I eventually set out on the Summit loop already well past my original goal time. Any thought of racing for a particular time was gone. There was only one goal left: finish the damn race.

That final loop was torture. I was moving at a snail's pace and found myself watching every single kilometre slowly tick by. All the food and fluid I'd managed to get down at the aid stations eventually came back up at around 72K.

Strangely, I felt better immediately after vomiting. I drank some water and kept moving, only to vomit again at around 76K.

By then it was dark, and thankfully I had my headlamp with me. Running at night was actually one of the more enjoyable parts of those final kilometres because the temperature had finally cooled off. Unfortunately, after more than 70K on my legs, the hills and technical terrain were painful.

I eventually crossed the finish line feeling absolutely terrible, but I had finished my first 50 miler. Somehow, after everything that happened over the second half of the race, I also finished 3rd in my age category. That definitely made all the suffering feel a little more worthwhile.

Post-race

  • Finish Time: 12:26:59

  • Recovery Meal: Too sick to consume anything!

The first 45K of this race was a lot of fun. After that, it turned into 35K of survival with very little nutrition going in and multiple extended stops at aid stations.

The frustrating part was that my legs, feet, and body were actually fine. I never felt like the distance itself had broken me down. This race was almost entirely a battle with my gut and the heat.

I got back to the hotel and vomited again before eventually going to sleep. I also developed a heat rash across my neck and chest. Needless to say, recovery nutrition wasn't happening that night.

Lessons Learned

  • My body is durable enough to handle 80K. My gut was the limiting factor, not my legs or feet.
  • Heat and humidity continue to be strongly associated with my late-race nausea and vomiting. I need to better understand my cooling, hydration, sodium, and fueling needs in hot conditions.
  • Reducing my carbohydrate intake from the 90–95 g/hr I used at Moose to around 80 g/hr did not eliminate the GI problems. Carb intake alone may not be the cause.
